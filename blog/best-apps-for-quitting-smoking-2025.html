Best Apps for Quitting Smoking in 2025

Tools ⏱ 7 min read 📅 2025

Quit-smoking apps have come a long way. The right one combines real-time health tracking, on-demand support, behavior change tools, and motivation that doesn't feel gimmicky. Here's an honest rundown of the leading options in 2025.

What to Look For

  • Real health milestones, not generic countdowns
  • Craving support available the moment an urge hits
  • Money saved tracker — visual motivation matters
  • Habit tracking and journaling
  • Community or coaching
  • Reasonable pricing — most paid apps run $5–$15/month

Bloom

A 2025 SwiftUI-native app designed around emotional reward. Real-time health tracking, AI coach, Craving SOS with guided breathing, and a unique sakura garden that grows as you stay smoke-free. iOS only, with a free tier and Premium for advanced features. Strong choice for users who respond to visual progress and beautiful design.

Smoke Free

One of the most established apps. Tracks health milestones, money saved, cravings, and offers daily missions. Has been around long enough to have strong data backing its approach. Available on iOS and Android. Pro features behind a one-time purchase.

QuitNow!

Solid free option with a global community feature — useful if you want peer support without paying. Achievement-based motivation. Available cross-platform.

Quit Genius

Closer to a digital therapeutic — CBT-based program with structured coursework and optional coaching. More clinically rigorous than most. Higher price point. Best for serious quitters who respond to structured programs.

EasyQuit

Simple, no-frills tracker. Good for users who want milestone-tracking without bells and whistles.

Kwit

Gamified approach with achievements and motivational cards. Polished design. Strong on iOS and Android.

What App to Pick

  • If you respond to visual progress: Bloom or Kwit
  • If you want clinical structure: Quit Genius
  • If you want a free, established option: Smoke Free or QuitNow
  • If you want peer community: QuitNow
  • If you want AI coaching on demand: Bloom

Do Apps Actually Help?

Research suggests app users have measurably higher quit success rates than unassisted quitters. The mechanism isn't magic — it's accountability, visible progress, and just-in-time support during cravings. The app effect roughly doubles bare-willpower outcomes.

Apps Aren't Magic

The best app for you is the one you'll actually open every day. The features matter less than consistency. Pick one. Commit. Don't app-hop.
