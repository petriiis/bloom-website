The 4-7-8 Breathing Technique for Cravings

Cravings ⏱ 5 min read 📅 2025

The 4-7-8 technique is the closest thing to a craving off-switch. It takes one minute. It requires no equipment. It can be done at your desk, in your car, or in the bathroom at a party. And it works, fast.

The Technique

  1. Sit upright. Place the tip of your tongue against the ridge behind your upper front teeth.
  2. Exhale completely through your mouth, making a "whoosh" sound.
  3. Close your mouth and inhale through your nose for 4 seconds.
  4. Hold your breath for 7 seconds.
  5. Exhale through your mouth for 8 seconds, "whoosh" sound.
  6. Repeat 4 cycles.

Total time: about 76 seconds.

Why It Works

4-7-8 activates the parasympathetic nervous system, your body's "rest and digest" mode. The long exhale increases vagal tone, slowing heart rate and lowering cortisol. The breath hold raises CO2 slightly, which improves oxygen delivery and triggers a relaxation response.

For cravings specifically, the technique works because:

  • It mimics the deep inhale of a cigarette without nicotine
  • It gives you a structured 60-second pause, longer than the peak of most cravings
  • It activates the same calming response smokers attribute to cigarettes
  • It interrupts the automatic craving-to-action loop

When to Use It

  • The moment a craving hits
  • Before a known trigger situation (a meeting, a phone call, a drive)
  • When you feel restless or anxious
  • Before bed if quit-related insomnia is keeping you up

Tips for Doing It Right

  • The numbers matter less than the ratio, exhale should be twice as long as inhale
  • If 7 seconds is too long, scale to 3-5-6 and work up
  • You may feel lightheaded the first few times, that's normal, just slow down
  • Practice it daily even when you don't have cravings, it becomes more effective with reps

What the Science Says

Research on slow paced breathing shows measurable reductions in cortisol, blood pressure, and self-reported anxiety after just a few minutes. Studies on smoking specifically find that brief breathwork reduces craving intensity by 25–40% compared to control conditions.

Pair It With Other Tools

Use 4-7-8 as your first response, then layer: a glass of water, a walk, a piece of gum. The breath buys you 60 seconds to choose, and 60 seconds is usually all you need.
