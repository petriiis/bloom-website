Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT): A Complete Guide

Methods ⏱ 7 min read 📅 2025

Nicotine replacement therapy delivers controlled doses of nicotine without the tar, carbon monoxide, and 7,000 other chemicals in cigarettes. Done right, NRT roughly doubles your chance of quitting successfully.

How NRT Works

The goal is simple: keep nicotine in your bloodstream at a low, steady level so withdrawal symptoms stay manageable, then gradually taper down. NRT breaks the link between nicotine and the behaviors (smoking, lighting, inhaling) that reinforce addiction.

The Five NRT Options

Patches

A steady, slow-release dose delivered through the skin over 16 or 24 hours. Best for: baseline nicotine coverage. Comes in 21mg, 14mg, and 7mg strengths for stepping down. Apply to a clean, hairless spot — rotate sites to avoid irritation.

Gum

Fast-acting nicotine absorbed through the cheek lining. Best for: breakthrough cravings. Comes in 2mg and 4mg. Chew slowly, park between cheek and gum, repeat. Don't chew like normal gum — that releases nicotine too fast and causes nausea.

Lozenges

Similar to gum but dissolved slowly. Best for people who don't like chewing. Same 2mg/4mg strengths, same absorption profile.

Inhalers (prescription)

A plastic mouthpiece that delivers nicotine vapor. Best for: smokers who need to replace the hand-to-mouth ritual.

Nasal Spray (prescription)

The fastest-acting NRT, closest to a cigarette in delivery speed. Best for: heavy smokers with intense cravings.

The Combination Approach

Research is now clear: combining a long-acting patch with a short-acting product (gum or lozenge) for breakthrough cravings is more effective than either alone. The patch handles baseline; the gum handles spikes.

Dosage Guidelines

  • 10+ cigarettes/day or first cigarette within 30 minutes of waking → start at 21mg patch + 4mg gum/lozenge as needed
  • Under 10/day → 14mg patch + 2mg gum/lozenge

Use for 8–12 weeks, then step down gradually.

Common Mistakes

  • Under-dosing. Most people use too little NRT and assume it doesn't work.
  • Stopping too early. Eight weeks minimum.
  • Smoking while wearing a patch. Don't — this can deliver too much nicotine.
  • Treating gum like Bubblicious. Chew-park-chew-park, slowly.

Is NRT Safe?

Yes. Even long-term use is far safer than continued smoking. The danger from cigarettes comes from combustion byproducts, not nicotine itself. Talk to a doctor if you're pregnant, have heart disease, or take other medications.

NRT Plus Behavior Change

NRT handles chemistry. It doesn't handle the morning-coffee cigarette habit or the stress-relief ritual. Pair NRT with behavior change — a quit app, a coach, replacement routines — and your odds go way up.
