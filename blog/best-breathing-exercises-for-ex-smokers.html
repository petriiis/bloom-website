Best Breathing Exercises for Ex-Smokers

Health ⏱ 6 min read 📅 2025

Smoking damages lungs not just at the alveolar level but also at the level of how you breathe. Most smokers develop shallow, chest-dominant breathing patterns. After quitting, retraining your breath rebuilds lung capacity faster and provides a powerful craving-management tool.

Why Breathwork Matters for Ex-Smokers

  • Strengthens respiratory muscles (intercostals, diaphragm)
  • Improves oxygen exchange efficiency
  • Helps clear remaining mucus and debris
  • Activates parasympathetic nervous system (calming)
  • Manages cravings in real time
  • Reduces anxiety during early quitting

Exercise 1: Diaphragmatic (Belly) Breathing

The foundation. Most smokers chest-breathe, which is shallow and inefficient.

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent
  2. One hand on chest, one on belly
  3. Inhale through nose so only the belly hand rises
  4. Exhale through pursed lips, belly falls
  5. 5–10 minutes daily

Exercise 2: Pursed-Lip Breathing

Particularly helpful for smokers with reduced lung function, even mildly. Slows breathing rate and keeps airways open longer.

  1. Inhale through nose for 2 seconds
  2. Purse lips like blowing out a candle
  3. Exhale slowly through pursed lips for 4 seconds
  4. Repeat 5–10 times

Exercise 3: 4-7-8 Breathing

The craving killer. (Inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8.) Activates parasympathetic system and creates a structured 60-second pause that outlasts most cravings.

Exercise 4: Box Breathing

Used by Navy SEALs for stress regulation. Simple and powerful.

  1. Inhale 4 seconds
  2. Hold 4 seconds
  3. Exhale 4 seconds
  4. Hold 4 seconds
  5. Repeat 4–6 cycles

Exercise 5: Three-Part Breath (Yogic)

Builds full lung capacity by training all three regions to expand.

  1. Inhale, filling the lower belly first
  2. Continue inhaling, expanding the ribs
  3. Finish inhaling, lifting the upper chest
  4. Exhale in reverse order, chest, ribs, belly
  5. Slow and deliberate, 5–10 cycles

Optional Tool: Breath Training Devices

Inspiratory muscle trainers (Powerbreathe, AirPhysio) provide resistance training for the respiratory muscles. Research shows meaningful lung function improvements with 5–10 minutes of daily use. Particularly helpful for long-term smokers with reduced capacity.

Recommended Daily Practice

  • Morning: 5 minutes diaphragmatic breathing
  • Throughout day: 4-7-8 or box breathing as cravings hit
  • Evening: 5 minutes three-part breath or pursed-lip breathing

Total: about 15 minutes per day. The lung capacity gains within 4 weeks are typically measurable.

Sign That It's Working

  • You can take a deeper breath than 2 weeks ago
  • Stairs that winded you don't anymore
  • Cravings respond to a single round of 4-7-8
  • You're sleeping more soundly
  • Anxiety baseline is lower

The Reward

Within a few months of consistent breath training, most ex-smokers report breath quality better than they remember having even before they started smoking. That's not nostalgia, it's a measurable change.
