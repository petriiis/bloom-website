Smoking and Pregnancy: Risks and Quitting Support

Health ⏱ 7 min read 📅 2025

Every cigarette you smoke while pregnant, your baby is exposed to nicotine and carbon monoxide through the only oxygen source it has. There's no safe amount. And quitting at any point in the pregnancy — even late — still makes a real, measurable difference.

Why Smoking During Pregnancy Is Harmful

Every cigarette delivers nicotine and carbon monoxide to the placenta, the only oxygen source your baby has. Nicotine constricts the placental blood vessels. Carbon monoxide reduces oxygen delivery. The combination starves the developing baby.

Specific Risks

  • Miscarriage: Risk increases roughly 1.5–2× in smokers
  • Stillbirth: Risk increases 23–47%
  • Preterm birth: Up to 2× more likely
  • Low birth weight: Babies of smokers weigh on average 200g less
  • SIDS: Triples the risk
  • Placental complications: Higher rates of placenta previa and placental abruption
  • Birth defects: Increased risk of cleft lip/palate and other defects
  • Long-term effects: Children of smokers have higher rates of asthma, attention disorders, and respiratory illness

The Good News: Quitting Helps Immediately

Even quitting in the second or third trimester significantly reduces risk. Quitting before week 15 returns most outcomes to near non-smoker levels. Carbon monoxide clears within 24 hours of your last cigarette, meaning your baby has access to dramatically more oxygen by day 2.

What's Safe for Quitting During Pregnancy

Behavioral Support, First Line

Counseling, quit lines, and apps are the first recommendation. They work, pregnancy-tailored programs have meaningfully higher success rates than generic.

Nicotine Replacement Therapy

NRT during pregnancy is a careful conversation with your OB. Current guidance from major medical bodies: NRT is generally considered safer than continued smoking, but should be used at the lowest effective dose and under medical supervision. Patches deliver less nicotine spike than gum or lozenges. Discuss with your provider.

What to Avoid

  • Bupropion and varenicline are generally avoided in pregnancy
  • Vaping is not recommended, long-term fetal effects are unknown and nicotine alone harms development
  • "Cutting back" without quitting is not protective, even light smoking causes harm

Talking to Your Provider

Be honest. Many smokers underreport to OBs out of guilt. Your doctor's role is to help, not judge, and accurate information helps them protect your baby. Most providers will help you build a quit plan immediately.

For Partners

If your partner is pregnant and you smoke, the most supportive thing you can do is quit with her. At minimum, smoke far from her, never inside, and change your clothes before contact.

The Bottom Line

Every cigarette not smoked during pregnancy is a measurable benefit to your baby. Quit support is widely available, call 1-800-QUIT-NOW in the US, or your country's equivalent. The pregnancy is the most powerful motivation you'll ever have. Use it.
